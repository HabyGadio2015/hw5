Every claim below is taken from this page's ticket tabs, the Cash tab, and output/audit_trail.json, specifically entries 1–29 (the Problem 9 live runs). (1) How did the agents perform on each ticket, and why?

  • Ticket 101: strong. Every fact matched the database. CC-TEE-WHITE size S was 0 in stock. Bulldog Print Co was blocked by invoice #501 (can_ship: false). The $840 invoice was overdue against shop date 2026-08-31 and affordable against $3,400. ACCOUNTING produced a correct payment_proposal and also raised a caution nobody had asked for: the invoice does not say how many shirts the $840 covers. After Haby approved the payment (Cash tab: $3,400 → $2,560), BOSS still reported Not Resolved. That was honest: the vendor was unblocked, but this system has no tool to order or receive a restock. The weak spot was redundancy. In run 1, ACCOUNTING was started twice, once directly by BOSS and once through INVENTORY. In run 2, CUSTOMER SERVICE repeated the vendor check that ACCOUNTING had just done.
  • Ticket 102: weakest. The facts were right: $2,400 rent due 2026-09-02, not overdue, and the agents worked out themselves that paying it would leave $160 of the $2,560. Refusing to propose a payment while unsure about a duplicate does honor Shop Rule 2. But run 1 took 11 logged agent runs, with BOSS called back in by its own delegates. get_ticket ran in all 11, and get_lease and get_shop_today ran in 10. No hop added new information. In run 2, ACCOUNTING never called check_payment_history, even though the tool had been added for exactly this blocker and its prompt told it to use it. The tool would have answered "no payment recorded." The right move was to propose the $2,400 with that caveat and let the human decide. Two runs ended with no progress and no cash moved.
  • Ticket 103: good. The shortfall math was correct (8 of 20, short 12) and so was the pricing ($22 cost, $58 list). The team also saw that the vendor was no longer blocked, which fits ticket 101's payment earlier in the same session. ACCOUNTING set the Shop Rule 1 floor with the math: break-even is $22 per hoodie, and at list price the 20 hoodies total $1,160 with $720 gross profit, so any discount must keep the price above $22. BOSS's summary and CUSTOMER SERVICE's draft both repeat that condition. The team did not invent a discount number. CUSTOMER SERVICE drafted a message asking Yale AI Club for their target price. The ticket stayed Not Resolved because the shortfall remains and the customer has not answered.

(2) How did Actual compare to the Expected plan?

  • 101: close match. The predicted chain BOSS → INVENTORY → ACCOUNTING (plus CUSTOMER SERVICE) happened. BOSS also went to ACCOUNTING directly. Every tool in the plan ran (get_ticket, check_inventory, list_vendors, check_vendor_invoice_status, get_invoice, get_cash_balance, save_draft). ACCOUNTING also ran get_pricing and get_shop_today, which the plan did not predict. One prediction was wrong: the plan had INVENTORY calling check_vendor_invoice_status, but INVENTORY handed that check to ACCOUNTING. As planned, no agent ever executed record_payment. The only payment came through the human-approval route. The verdict matched: Not Resolved.
  • 102: biggest gap. The plan expected a clean BOSS → FACILITIES → ACCOUNTING chain ending in a $2,400 proposal waiting for approval. Instead, the agents looped (BOSS ↔ ACCOUNTING ↔ FACILITIES) and proposed nothing. The plan's cross-ticket cash warning was right: paying both bills leaves only $160, and the agents computed $160 themselves. The plan's rule "BOSS calls INVENTORY first" turned out not to be universal. On this rent ticket BOSS skipped INVENTORY entirely, which was correct because the ticket has no SKU.
  • 103: matched on facts, differed on approach. The plan's chain BOSS → INVENTORY → ACCOUNTING → CUSTOMER SERVICE happened, along with 8 of 20 in stock and the $22 / $58 prices. Two predictions were wrong. (a) The plan expected the restock to be blocked by invoice #501, but ticket 101's approved payment had already unblocked the vendor. Running the tickets in order created a dependency the plan missed. (b) As the plan expected, ACCOUNTING did set a specific floor ($22, with the $1,160 / $720 math). What the plan missed is the next step: no agent proposed an actual discounted price. CUSTOMER SERVICE asked the customer for their target price instead. The plan also said update_ticket_status would not fire. BOSS did use it, on 102 and 103, but only to set partial, never resolved, so Shop Rule 7 held.

(3) What would have been simpler as one agent with tools, and why?

All three tickets. Each was a short, straight line through a tiny database (9 tables, 3 tickets), and none needed specialists working in parallel. With 5 agents, every hop restarted from nothing and fetched the same facts again. Ticket 102 shows this most clearly: 11 agent runs, get_ticket 11 times, get_lease 10 times. On ticket 101, check_inventory ran 3 times in run 1 alone (INVENTORY, ACCOUNTING, CUSTOMER SERVICE). One agent with the same 13 tools would fetch each fact once, reason over all of it together, and use a fraction of the requests. The 12-request test in Problem 5 ran out mid-delegation, so the real runs were given a 40-request budget. Delegation also caused 102's failure: each hop handed the question "is rent already paid?" to the next agent instead of answering it with the tool. The split did buy two things. Each prompt stays focused (ACCOUNTING's money rules live in one place), and the audit trail shows which "department" said what. But the safety guarantees that matter are enforced in code, not by having 5 agents: record_payment refuses without approved_by, refuses a negative balance, and refuses a duplicate, and it is filtered out of every agent's toolset. One agent working under those same tools would be exactly as safe.

(4) Three new Campus Customs problems this team could solve with the tools built

  • A mug restock. CC-MUG-CREST is 0 in stock today (OS size). This is the same pattern as ticket 101: check_inventory → list_vendors (Elm City Gifts, "mugs and small goods", 3-day lead time) → check_vendor_invoice_status → get_invoice / get_cash_balance → payment_proposal → Haby approves through POST /payments/approve. With checking at $2,560 (Cash tab), any bill above that would be refused by record_payment rather than overdraw the account.
  • A cap order that can fully resolve today. CC-HAT-BLUE has 40 in stock, cost $6, list $22. An order for, say, 10 caps with a discount request needs no restock and no payment. check_inventory confirms 40 ≥ 10. get_pricing gives the $6 floor for Shop Rule 1. CUSTOMER SERVICE drafts the reply, and BOSS can truthfully set update_ticket_status(..., "resolved"). Unlike 101 and 103, nothing here is blocked by stock.
  • A duplicate-bill attempt. If a new ticket claimed invoice #501 was still owed, check_payment_history("invoice", 501) would return the real row: $840, paid 2026-08-31, approved by Haby. If anyone still tried to pay it, record_payment would refuse it as a duplicate. Both behaviors were tested in Problems 7 and 9.

(5) Three new problems this team could not solve, and what it would need

  • Actually restocking, which is why 101 and 103 are stuck. No tool places or receives an order, and shop time never moves forward, so the 5-day lead time can never pass. Needed: a purchase_orders table, plus create_purchase_order(vendor_id, sku, size, qty) (human-approved, like payments; ticket 103's 12 hoodies at $22 cost = $264), receive_shipment(po_id) to add to inventory.qty, and advance_shop_date(days) on desk. Owners: INVENTORY, or a new PURCHASING agent, with ACCOUNTING approving the cost.
  • Monthly rent, a real flaw in what was built. record_payment and check_payment_history detect duplicates by (kind, ref_id). Once rent for lease 1 is paid one time, every later month's rent for lease 1 would be refused as a duplicate. Paying rent also never moves leases.next_due forward. Needed: a billing-period field (or a rent_bills table with one row per month), duplicate checks keyed on that period, and a FACILITIES tool that sets the next due date after payment.
  • Closing a sale. Revenue is not modeled, and cash only goes out (Cash tab: the only movement is −$840). So even if Yale AI Club agrees to a price on ticket 103, nothing can record the sale, take hoodies out of stock, or add cash, and no draft can actually be sent. Needed: record_sale(ticket_id, sku, size, qty, unit_price), which takes the units out of inventory, adds cash, and refuses any price at or below unit_cost (Shop Rule 1 in code); a revenue entry in the cash ledger; and a human-approved send_message(ticket_id), a step that is deliberately missing today under Shop Rule 5. A SALES agent, or an expanded CUSTOMER SERVICE, would own it.
